What Antigravity is struggling with is that your current markdown is describing:

WHAT EXISTS

instead of

WHAT CHANGES

Rendering engines are actually much better at building scenes than building transitions.

The markdown tells it:

* there is a warp tunnel
* there is a planet
* there is plasma
* there is a storm
* there is a monolith

But the viewer never experiences:

warp → planet
planet → atmosphere
atmosphere → fire
fire → cloud
cloud → ocean
ocean → monolith

Those transitions are where the emotion lives.

The biggest thing I would feed Antigravity is not object descriptions.

It is temporal choreography.

HIGH-FIDELITY CINEMATIC CHOREOGRAPHY
DO NOT TREAT AS SEPARATE SHOTS

This entire sequence is one continuous camera ride.

The camera is physically attached near the engine section of the rocket.

The camera never teleports.

The camera never jumps to another viewpoint.

The rocket remains visible during the entire sequence.

The audience must always understand where they are.

The scene should feel like being strapped to the outside of the ship.

SCENE X

FRAME X-001

Normal space.

Stars are visible.

Rocket hull is dark.

Chrome surface reflects tiny stars.

Nothing is moving.

The audience should feel anticipation.

Like sitting in a race car before launch.

The rocket occupies the right side of frame.

The left side contains deep space.

No vortex exists.

No distortion exists.

No motion blur exists.

No camera vibration exists.
